## 1. Scout — 3D / LiDAR ground and road geometry the pipeline already computes (Stage 1 plane fitting, its persistence, and what Stages 5/6/8/9 do with it)

**Summary.** Stage 1 is the only place in the pipeline that computes ground geometry. Per keyframe it fits 8 azimuthal-sector planes of the form z = a·x + b·y + d by seeded RANSAC on the ego-motion-compensated ACCUMULATED (LiDAR-only) cloud, polishes each on its consensus set, guards against tilt > 15 deg and inlier ratio < 0.35 by substituting a whole-cloud least-squares plane, then APPLIES the planes to the fused single-sweep cloud to delete every point with |z − ground(x,y)| < 0.30 m. The fitted plane coefficients ARE persisted — all 8 per keyframe, with quality fields and the rejected fit verbatim — inside `<work_root>/stage1_ingestion/scenes/<scene>/filter_diagnostics.json`. What is NOT persisted is any ground point mask or index: ground membership is computed transiently in `ingest_keyframe` and only the survivors are written to `.pcd.bin`. Nothing downstream ever reads those planes: Stages 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 contain zero references to `sector_planes` or `filter_diagnostics.json`, Stage 6 takes box height from the raw cluster z-extent, and Stage 8 states in its own manifest that no ground plane is consulted. Critically, on THIS substrate the fitted plane is not the road: LIDAR_TOP's `calibrated_sensor` is identity, so ego z = 0 sits at the sensor while the road sits at z ≈ −2.15 m, and the RANSAC candidate band of z ∈ [−1.5, +1.5] (whose provenance note assumes "z=0 at ground") excludes the road entirely. All 18,416 persisted planes have d ∈ [−0.72, 0.0]; not one is anywhere near the road. 76.4 % of sector fits were rejected and replaced by the global plane, which is why Stage 1's marker is `_SUCCESS.degraded`.

### Implications

- A road-surface stage CANNOT reuse the persisted sector planes as a road surface. They are persisted, cheap to read, and geometrically well-formed — but on this substrate they sit ~1.9 m above the actual road (median d = -0.228 m vs road z ~ -2.15 m), and 76.4 % of them are not even sector fits but the substituted global plane. Treating them as the road would put a 3D road surface in the air, deterministically and invisibly.
- The stage would have to fit its own ground/road geometry. It can, however, reuse the entire scaffolding for free: sector_index() is a pure function of (x,y), fit_sector_planes()/SectorPlane/height_at()/ground_distance() are importable and model-free numpy, and _sector_rng gives the seeded determinism §1.9 requires. The cheapest honest design is a Stage-1-style fitter with a substrate-correct candidate band (or the /home/mt/dhakascenes/fix/overlay.py percentile-seeded recipe) rather than a new algorithm.
- The input data a road stage needs is already on disk and already correct. Because Stage 1's plane is too high, the road surface survives ground removal: 52.1 % of the kept single-sweep points for keyframe aacfcda... lie in the road band. So the stage can read Stage 1's `single_sweep_cloud` path straight off the I-3 KeyframeRecord and does not need to re-read the dataroot or re-apply T_ego_lidar.
- It must respect the fusion. The single-sweep cloud is 79.6 % ZED stereo, capped at 20 m, with per-point provenance in the ring column (0-3 lidar, 10/11 stereo). A road stage must decide explicitly, and record, whether it fits on lidar-only (ring < 10, ~20k pts/keyframe, full 30 m annulus) or the fused cloud (~98k pts, dense near-field, stereo error growing as range squared). Silently fitting the fused cloud makes the road plane a stereo-calibration artifact within 20 m.
- It must not re-apply T_ego_lidar. §1.1 rule 3 — Stage 1 applies it exactly once and every cross-stage quantity is ego frame at the LiDAR anchor time. Stage 5's docstring and lift_keyframe's frame assertion are the precedent for how to enforce that.
- If it lifts a 2D road mask into 3D, it must build the ray-to-ground intersection itself. project_lidar_to_image is forward-only; angular_footprint is the only K^-1 use and yields directions with no depth and no ego-pose hop. Re-implementing the projection chain is forbidden by §1.3 (`conventions.project_lidar_to_image` is the one implementation) — so the correct move is to paint EXISTING cloud points with the road mask exactly as Stage 5 does, rather than unprojecting pixels.
- It can reuse Stage 5's per-keyframe npz `visible_point_index` (72,287 of 82,767 points for the sampled keyframe) as a precomputed 'this point lands in some camera' set, avoiding a second full projection pass — provided it accepts Stage 5's ground-filtered cloud and its 8-camera contest semantics.
- It must consume upstream through require_upstream() and will be forced to pass accept_degraded_upstream, because Stage 1 currently completes DEGRADED (rejection_rate 0.764). That acceptance has to be recorded in the new stage's own run_manifest per §1.7.
- It must write the three-state marker via write_marker/clear_markers (clear BEFORE the first byte), write run_manifest.json, and use write_json_atomic / write_jsonl_atomic — and it must exit 2 and write nothing on an upstream contract breach.
- Membership in the evaluation region must go through eval_region.in_region() and nowhere else; the stage may not hardcode a radius or wedge. Any road-area or coverage metric it publishes is a density under R2 with r_max 30 m, which must stay pinned to IngestConfig.range_cap_m.
- Every 2D quantity it emits must be absolute pixels at 1280x720 (IMAGE_WIDTH_PX/IMAGE_HEIGHT_PX), and any mask it writes should follow Stage 4's bit-packed npz convention with __width_px__/__height_px__/__bit_packed__ in the file, since MaskFile asserts rather than assumes resolution.
- It must not touch Stage 9's drivable_term. That flag is locked off and gate.py:192-195 refuses any other value; a road stage that wants to feed a spatial gate has to go through a documented decision plus no_production_counterpart marking, not a config change.
- Adding a road class means touching the taxonomy and the class-space plumbing, which currently has no surface/stuff class at all — only object phrases. A 'stuff' class is a new kind of entity for this pipeline's I-4/I-5 records (AnnotationRecord is box-shaped), so the output contract is a genuinely new schema, not a reuse.

If a road stage produces a trustworthy per-sector road plane, Stage 1's ground removal, Stage 6's low-biased box heights, and Stage 8's downward growth all become correctable — but changing Stage 1's ground band would change every downstream point count, so the two must not be entangled in one change.

### Blockers

- The persisted ground plane is not the road. All 18,416 planes have d in [-0.72, 0.0] while the road sits at z ~ -2.15 m; zero planes are below -1.7. Any design that says 'reuse Stage 1's ground plane' is wrong on this substrate as it stands.
- The cause is a frame-contract mismatch that nothing in the repo records: docs/comprehensive.md:152 fixes the ego origin at the rear axle projected to GROUND, but LIDAR_TOP's calibrated_sensor is [0,0,0]/identity, so ego z=0 is the sensor. Every z-referenced constant in the pipeline (ransac_candidate_z_band_m (-1.5,1.5), height_cap_m 4.0) was chosen under the first reading and is applied under the second.
- No ground point index or mask is persisted. Ground membership exists only as counts. A road stage that wants 'which points were ground' must recompute it — which is possible (the planes and sector rule are both persisted and deterministic) but is a recomputation, not a read.
- No 2D->3D unprojection or ray-plane intersection helper exists. Projecting a road mask onto a ground plane would require new geometry code, and §1.3 forbids reimplementing the projection chain, so the design has to route through point painting or add a helper to conventions.py under that rule.
- No HD map and no drivable-area prior: the dataroot has no maps/ directory and map.json is an empty stub. There is no external road geometry to validate against.

- Is the -1.5/+1.5 candidate band vs. a sensor-origin ego frame a known, accepted defect or an undiscovered one? - Do the camera extrinsics support road-mask painting at the accuracy a road surface needs? export_meta.json flags CAM_BACK's extrinsic as 'the GA-01 drawing nominal, not a calibration' and CAM_BACK_LEFT's intrinsic as nominal, and the handover names target-based lidar-camera calibration as the largest remaining source of geometric error.

## 2. Scout — Class-space / taxonomy machinery (taxonomy configs, Stage 3 caption + phrase spans, class maps, release/CVAT category tables, and every phrase-keyed downstream consumer)

**Summary.** The pipeline has exactly one class space: the DEDUPLICATED, ORDERED set of natural-language prompt PHRASES produced by `Taxonomy.phrases` from a taxonomy YAML's `prompt_phrase:` block. That phrase string — not a dotted nuScenes category, not a COCO name — is the join key for every downstream stage: Stage 6's DBSCAN epsilon, Stage 8's dimension prior, Stage 9's 2x-prior spatial gate, the CVAT COCO `category_id`, the release 18-class map, and the eval reachability suppression. Two files hold the space today: `taxonomy_pilot_nuscenes.yaml` (v2, 15 categories -> 10 phrases) and `taxonomy_pilot_dhaka.yaml` (v3, 17 -> 12), which is a SUPERSET SIBLING, not a replacement: its first 10 phrases are v2's verbatim in v2's order, so v2's caption is a byte PREFIX of v3's and every arm-A `phrase_char_spans` entry stays valid unchanged — an invariant asserted at runtime in `merge.py` and in `tests/test_armb_configs.py`. The caption/span machinery is CHEAPER than it looks: `phrase_char_spans` are a property of the caption STRING (computed while concatenating, never by `str.find`), and the token-level `span_map` is already nullable — the closed-vocabulary YOLO adapter returns `None` and the record says `"span_map": null`. So a stuff phrase would load, caption, and span fine. What actually breaks is everything downstream of the phrase: `Proposals` demands N boxes with x1<x2 inside the image and one mask per box in order; Stage 4 runs a cross-camera IoA-NMS at 0.5 in ego azimuth/elevation that would delete a road region in 5 of 6 cameras; `priors.py` emits one dims/eps_bev block per taxonomy phrase; `AnnotationRecord` demands a positive `size_wlh_m`, a quaternion and an `instance_token`. Nothing in the repo references a drivable surface under any spelling; the only existing hook is Stage 9's `drivable_term`, hard-locked to `"off"` by a validate() error.

### Implications

- Do NOT extend the shared phrase taxonomy with a road/stuff phrase. Every consumer of `Taxonomy.phrases` treats a phrase as an instance class with a 3D box: `priors.py` will emit a dims/eps_bev block for it (priors.py:568-580), Stage 8 will look up a dimension prior, Stage 9 will apply a 2x-BEV-prior spatial gate, and `release_category_map.yaml` will refuse the export unless the phrase is mapped into the 18 instance classes (export_release.py:178-183). None of those has a meaning for an amorphous region.
- The caption/prefix machinery is NOT the obstacle and must not be broken. If a road phrase ever does enter a caption, it can only be APPENDED — reordering or inserting invalidates v2's byte-prefix relationship and thus every archived arm-A `phrase_char_spans` (merge.py:221-227, tests/test_armb_configs.py:50-56). Verified: appending 'the road surface' to v3 keeps the prefix and yields a valid span (159,175).
- Give the road stage its OWN class space file, in the taxonomy-file SHAPE but a separate document — e.g. `configs/road_classes_dhaka.yaml` — plus its own source-vocabulary bridge in the `coco_to_phrase`/`excluded_*` pattern if a closed-vocabulary segmenter (Cityscapes/Mapillary/BDD100K heads) is used. That pattern is already vocabulary-agnostic and proven twice (oiv7, rsud20k), and `load_class_map` will enforce collapse-only against whatever taxonomy it is bound to.
- If the road stage's class names must ever coexist with object phrases in one export, namespace them the way arm B namespaced its pseudo-categories (`dhaka.cycle_rickshaw`), e.g. `surface.road`, and remember `PromptConfig` will reject that string as a dotted category if it is ever used as a PROMPT (model_interfaces.py:142,277-283). Keep the dotted key on the CATEGORY side and a plain-language value on the phrase side, exactly as the existing files do.
- Do not route road output through the `proposal_2d` role. `Proposals` requires (N,4) ordered boxes inside the image with one mask per box in order (model_interfaces.py:416-442, 353-372); a single amorphous region has no defensible box and one region per image would be a degenerate N=1 with a near-full-frame box that the Stage 4 cross-camera IoA-NMS at 0.5 would then delete in 5 of 6 cameras (masks.py:355-402). Declare a NEW role rather than bending this one.
- Do not route road output through `AnnotationRecord`. It demands `instance_token`, positive `size_wlh_m`, a unit quaternion and a single-sweep `num_lidar_pts` basis (schemas.py:855-905). A road stage needs its own record type (or a mask/polygon artifact plus a manifest) and must say so, rather than emitting a synthetic box.
- Reuse the existing mask container conventions if the stage writes 2D masks: Stage 4's per-keyframe bit-packed npz storing `__width_px__`/`__height_px__`/`__bit_packed__` and asserting them against the pipeline resolution (lift.py:319-342). But note that container is indexed by proposal position, so a road stage needs a distinct key convention, not proposal indices.
- Honour the three-state marker and manifest discipline exactly: write `run_manifest.json`, call `require_upstream()` on Stage 1 (and Stage 3 only if it consumes proposals), record `marker.degraded`/`marker.causes` in its own provenance, and exit 2 on a contract break with nothing written (manifest.py:254-303). Exit 1 for a real degradation such as a frame where the surface model could not be fit.
- Record the reachability story the way `class_space.py` does: if the road segmenter's source vocabulary cannot reach a declared road class, list it as unreachable in the manifest and make eval suppress-and-count rather than score a structural zero (class_space.py:1-33). Also add the new provider to `OPEN_VOCABULARY_PROVIDERS` or to the class-map path, or `class_space.py` will treat it as a provider whose class space nobody has established (class_space.py:49-53).
- Do not silently reuse `configs/taxonomy_pilot_nuscenes.yaml` as a default. `class_space.py:45-46` and `priors.py:793` and `export_cvat_coco.py:60` all hard-default to the v2 file; a new stage that defaults the same way will describe a run that never happened on a merged tree. Follow `run_stages.sh:534-546`'s `taxonomy_for_tree()` precedent and thread the class-space file per tree.
- If the road stage is meant to feed Stage 9's `spatial_ok` drivable term, that is a DECISION that must be taken explicitly: `GateConfig.validate()` currently returns an error for any value but 'off' (gate.py:192-196), and enabling it requires a real region lookup plus `no_production_counterpart` marking and a populated `spatial_ok_source` (schemas.py:778-784). Enabling it as a flag is explicitly forbidden.
- Stage 1 is where the 3D road surface already lives, and it is lossy: the ground points are deleted (ingest.py:719-740) and only the 8 per-sector plane coefficients survive (ingest.py:808, verified on disk for 2302 keyframes). A 3D road stage must either consume those planes, re-read raw LiDAR from the read-only dataroot, or require a Stage 1 change to persist a ground mask — the third option changes an upstream fingerprint and every downstream marker.
- If road classes ever reach the CVAT exporters, insert them so they SORT LAST or accept a category-id renumbering: ids come from `sorted(set(phrases))` (export_cvat_coco.py:72-76). Measured: 'the road surface' renumbers nothing, 'a road surface' renumbers 4 existing classes, 'a drivable area' renumbers 8. Also note there is no `iscrowd`/RLE path in the exporter — a stuff region would need one, and CVAT label schemas are fixed at project creation (cvat_setup.py:11,218-226).
- Beware the lexical collision if a road phrase ever shares the caption with `a road barrier`: the C22 measurement is that under max-over-tokens 'a road barrier' already fired on bare road surface via the token *road* (proposals.py:737-744). The current default `mean_over_content_tokens` mitigates this, but the two phrases would share a content token and any regression to max-over-tokens re-opens a measured defect.

### Blockers

- No stuff/surface/semantic class exists anywhere in the repo. Verified absent under every spelling: driveable, driveable_surface, drivable_area, road_segment, ped_crossing, walkway, lidarseg, stuff -> 0 files each. There is no prior art in-tree to extend; the road stage's class space has to be authored from nothing.
- There is NO ground truth on the current substrate to validate a road segmentation against, and no map. /home/mt/dhakascenes/data/pilot_1632/v1.0-dhaka-fixed/{category,instance,sample_annotation,attribute,visibility}.json are all 3 bytes ('[]'), and there is no maps/ directory. Any road-surface quality claim on this data would be unmeasured, and the pipeline's own eval machinery (eval_2d/eval_3d/export_gt_coco) is GT-driven.
- The 3D ground returns are destroyed upstream. Stage 1 deletes all points within 0.30 m of its fitted sector planes and persists only the filtered clouds (ingest.py:719-744). A LiDAR-side road stage cannot be added downstream of Stage 1 without either re-reading the dataroot or changing Stage 1 — and changing Stage 1 invalidates every downstream marker and fingerprint.
- `Proposals` and `AnnotationRecord` have no representation for a non-instance region: Proposals requires ordered boxes plus one mask per box in order (model_interfaces.py:416-442, 353-372); AnnotationRecord requires instance_token + positive size_wlh_m + quaternion (schemas.py:855-905). A road stage cannot emit under either contract without fabricating geometry, which is exactly the class of silent-wrongness this pipeline is built to refuse.
- Stage 4's cross-camera IoA-NMS at 0.5 in ego azimuth/elevation (masks.py:355-402) is designed to delete duplicate views of one object. A road surface visible in every ring camera IS the same physical region in every camera; running it through this contest would delete 5 of 6 views by design, and disabling the contest for one class would change the arbitration semantics for all.
- Stage 9's drivable term — the one natural consumer of a road region — is LOCKED OFF by a validate() error (gate.py:192-196) with the lock recorded as '§11 decision 7, locked at Phase 10'. Wiring the road stage into it requires reopening a locked decision, not a config change.
- `release_category_map.yaml` has 18 classes, all instance objects, and the exporter refuses any unmapped source string and any target outside `classes` (export_release.py:178-207). A road class has no landing place in the release format, and `unresolved:` already demonstrates the project's convention: an unmade taxonomy decision is a hard refusal, not a default.
- The pilot's per-class thresholds are still `{}` in both taxonomy files with the inherited unvalidated 0.40 default (taxonomy_pilot_nuscenes.yaml:136-144, taxonomy_pilot_dhaka.yaml:64-70). Any new class space will inherit the same untuned-threshold caveat, and the v3 file already records tuning as owed BEFORE any label-quality claim.

### Open questions

- Is the road stage's output meant to be 2D (per-camera mask / polygon on the image plane), 3D (a surface model or ground point set in ego frame), or both? Stage 1 already produces a 3D answer (8 sector planes per keyframe) and nothing consumes it; the design should say whether the new stage supersedes, consumes or duplicates that.
- Is it one class ('road surface') or a small semantic set (road / sidewalk / lane markings / unpaved)? Nothing in-tree constrains this, and the answer determines whether a class-space FILE is even needed or a single hardcoded constant suffices.
- Where does it sit in the chain? It has no dependency on Stage 3 proposals, so it could branch off Stage 1 directly and run in parallel with 3/3b/3f/3m — but `run_stages.sh` step ordering, and the question of whether Stage 4 or Stage 9 consumes it, are unsettled.
- Does the road output need to reach CVAT for human verification? If yes, the exporter needs an `iscrowd`/RLE or polygon path for a stuff region and a separate CVAT project (label schemas are fixed at creation, cvat_setup.py:218-226), and the decision of whether it shares a project with the object pre-labels is unmade.
- Does the road output need to reach the release format at all? `release_category_map.yaml`'s 18 classes have no surface entry and `comprehensive.md` §6.1's frozen 16-class taxonomy has none either; whether the road layer is a released artifact or an internal QA input changes whether the taxonomy freeze has to be reopened.
- Was the Stage 9 drivable term (§11 decision 7) locked because no map existed, or because the pipeline did not want a spatial gate at all? gate.py:156-161 gives the first reason ('the map expansion is on disk so it IS implementable... a pilot component with no production counterpart teaches nothing'), which suggests a road stage with a real Dhaka-side producer would REMOVE the objection — but the decision text says 'locked at Phase 10' and I cannot verify from the repo whether that lock is reopenable.
- How would a road segmentation be evaluated on this substrate given there is no GT at all? Either a hand-annotated seed set is owed, or the stage ships with an explicitly-recorded 'unmeasured' status in the style of the pipeline's existing untuned-threshold caveats.
- Does the intended segmenter carry its own fixed vocabulary (Cityscapes/Mapillary/BDD100K), and if so should the existing `load_class_map` + `assert_covers` mechanism be reused verbatim to bind it? That would give the road stage the same wrong-checkpoint protection for free, but requires the checkpoint to expose a `model.names`-equivalent.

## 3. Scout — Stage 4 (pipeline/stage4_masks/masks.py) — 2D box-prompted segmentation: on-disk representation, provider abstraction, box↔mask binding, batching/VRAM/runtime, upstream contract; and what a road-surface "stuff" stage would collide with

**Summary.** Stage 4 is a strictly per-instance, box-prompted segmenter. For each keyframe it loops the 8 camera rows of one Stage 3/3b proposals.jsonl keyframe, decodes the JPEG, and makes exactly ONE adapter.segment() call per camera image passing that image's whole box array; the adapter must return an (N,H,W) bool array with one mask per box in the same order at exactly 1280x720, and that count/order/resolution contract is asserted three separate times (inside each adapter, in MaskResult.validate/_check_masks, and again via result.assert_valid in the driver). Masks are stored as one np.savez_compressed npz per keyframe keyed by camera channel, bit-packed with np.packbits along the width axis (720x160 uint8 per mask), with __width_px__/__height_px__/__bit_packed__ header arrays — no RLE, no PNG, no polygon anywhere in the stage (polygons appear only in the CVAT exporter via cv2.findContours). A sibling masks.jsonl carries one row per keyframe whose `candidates` array is the per-instance ledger: channel, proposal_index, class_name, score, proposal box, mask tight box, n_mask_px, ego-frame angular footprint, C27 provenance, and the cross-camera IoA-NMS verdict (kept / suppressed_by / suppression_ioa). The array index inside the npz IS the Stage 3 proposal index — empty/tiny masks are dropped from `candidates` but deliberately retained as zero rows in the npz so indices never shift — and Stage 5 relies on exactly that, calling MaskFile.mask(channel, candidate["proposal_index"]). Providers are picked by a model_id→provider inference table with an explicit refusal for unknown checkpoints, dispatched through a 4-entry `_MASK_ADAPTERS` dict to three adapter classes (MobileSAM, transformers SAM2/SAM3, SAM 3.1 multiplex via Meta's sam3 package), each also registered under the `mask_2d` role in the shared registry. A full 2302-keyframe / 18416-image run took 3562 s (~59 min) on the 4090 with facebook/sam3.1 and 2084 s with facebook/sam3, producing 32 MB of masks.

### Implications

- A road stage CANNOT reuse Stage 4's output tree. The npz layout keys an array by camera channel and indexes it by Stage 3 proposal position; Stage 5, Stage 7, the CVAT exporter and the renderer all read `candidates[].proposal_index` as that array index. Appending one road region per image to `candidates` would give it a proposal_index that does not exist in any Stage 3 row, and Stage 5's `if inst.proposal_index >= masks.n_masks(channel)` guard (lift.py:467-471) would either refuse the run or, worse, silently index a real object's mask if the road region were inserted at a valid position. A road stage must write its OWN tree (e.g. work/stageN_road) with its own masks.jsonl-shaped index.
- A road stage still SHOULD copy Stage 4's storage idiom verbatim: one npz per keyframe, `<channel> -> (N,H,W)` bool bit-packed along the width axis with the __width_px__/__height_px__/__bit_packed__ header. It is the only mask representation any reader in this repo knows how to open (pipeline/stage5_lift/lift.py:319-372 MaskFile is imported by export_cvat_coco.py:38 and render_annotations.py:36), and reusing it means the exporter and renderer work with no new reader. For a single stuff region per image, N==1 and the semantics of the array index become 'class ordinal', not 'proposal index' — that reinterpretation must be stated in the row, not inferred, or MaskFile's docstring lies about what the index means.
- Storage is not a blocker but is ~an order of magnitude worse per mask. A trapezoidal road region at 1280x720 compresses to ~1.6 kB packed vs ~700 B for the average instance mask; 8 channels x 2302 keyframes estimates at ~29 MB — the same order as Stage 4's 32 MB. Measured: `1 packed road mask compressed bytes: 1572`, `8-channel keyframe npz bytes: 12406 -> 2302 keyframes MB: 28.6`.
- The provider abstraction generalizes only partially. `Mask2D.segment(images, boxes_xyxy_px, ...) -> MaskResult` is box-shaped in its signature AND in its validator: MaskResult.validate calls _check_masks with n_boxes, which errors with 'one mask per box, in order' (model_interfaces.py:362-363). A stuff segmenter that takes no boxes must EITHER declare a new role alongside MASK_2D (model_interfaces.py:118-121 ROLES, :856 ROLE_PROTOCOLS, :867 ROLE_METHODS) with its own payload dataclass and validator, OR call segment() with a 1-box array and accept that the contract is being read as 'one mask per prompt'. The former is cleaner and matches how the repo already handled a second facet (MaskVideoTracker at model_interfaces.py:778-820 is explicitly 'NOT a registry role' but a separately-declared protocol).
- Whatever provider a road stage uses, six registration points and the attribution guards are the pattern to imitate: an inference table with an explicit refusal for unknown checkpoints, an adapters dict, a provenance dict (indexed unconditionally, so a missing entry is a KeyError), a preflight branch that raises every CHEAP refusal BEFORE clear_markers, closed argparse choices, and register(). The preflight discipline (masks.py:1637-1746) is the load-bearing one: without it an exit-2 leaves a marker-less tree that every downstream reads as 'never ran'.
- Cross-camera IoA-NMS as written is an instance-duplicate suppressor and is meaningless for stuff: 'the same road' is legitimately visible from all 8 cameras and suppressing 7 of them would destroy the very coverage a road stage exists to produce. Empirically it would not fire on this rig anyway (max pairwise full-image angular IoA 0.437 < 0.5), but that is an accident of a 70-degree FOV, not a contract — a road stage must simply not run this contest, and must say so in its manifest rather than inheriting a suppression_rate field that means nothing.
- min_mask_px=16 and the empty/tiny drop rule invert for stuff: for instances a tiny mask is a defect; for a road region, a TINY mask is the defect and a MISSING one is the interesting event. A road stage needs its own degradation predicate (e.g. road fraction below a floor, or no road in CAM_FRONT) and must define what a degraded scene means, since Stage 4's 'n_kept == 0' rule (masks.py:2118-2122) would almost never fire.
- The road stage must NOT feed Stage 5. Stage 5 paints LiDAR into per-instance point sets and Stage 6 runs DBSCAN + L-shape fit per instance against a class BEV epsilon prior (cluster.py:781-787). A road region entering that chain becomes a single 'instance' of hundreds of thousands of ground points with no prior, which either refuses (on_missing_prior='refuse') or gets a fallback epsilon and produces a nonsense 3D box. Note also that Stage 1's clouds are ground-FILTERED for the >=5-return basis (schemas.py:857-861), so the very points a road region covers are the ones the object chain removes. A road stage is a sibling branch off Stage 1/Stage 3's image index, not a link in the 3D chain.
- Upstream choice matters: Stage 4's load_upstream is bound to a proposals-shaped tree (it dereferences upstream['prompt']['caption_sha256'] at masks.py:2188 and cross-checks image_size_px). A road stage does not need boxes at all — it needs the keyframe/sample_data index, so its natural upstream is stage1_ingestion (and it should pass current_fingerprint=metadata_fingerprint(paths) to require_upstream, which Stage 4 notably does NOT do). Reading Stage 3 instead would create a false dependency on a detector run.
- A road stage can run CONCURRENTLY with Stage 4 in wall-clock terms only if VRAM allows: Stage 4 already holds ~7.5 GiB peak under a 22000 MiB synthetic cap on the 4090 (manifest provenance for sam31_max_objects), and apply_vram_cap sets a per-process fraction, not a global one — two processes each taking their fraction of the same card will not be protected from each other. Sequential is the safe default, and the run_stages.sh step list would gain one more step (its parser at run_stages.sh:262 is a closed case list, and the clean-slate list at :355-366 plus the summary loop at :936 both need the new dir name).
- Runtime budget for a road stage: Stage 4 spends ~0.19 s/camera-image on SAM 3.1 with ~2.5 boxes/image, i.e. ~59 min for 18416 images. A one-region-per-image road segmenter over the same 18416 images at similar per-image cost lands in the same 30-60 min band; if only CAM_FRONT is segmented ('the road AHEAD'), 2302 images -> under 10 min. That choice — all 8 cameras vs front-only — is the single biggest cost lever and should be an explicit, recorded config value, not an implicit loop.
- The QA end of the pipeline already has a reserved, locked-off slot for exactly this signal: GateConfig.drivable_term='off' with a validator that refuses any other value, and GateVector.spatial_ok=None meaning 'not evaluated'. A road stage that wants to feed QA must go through that decision (§11 decision 7) rather than around it — the refusal text explicitly says enabling it 'requires a real map lookup plus no_production_counterpart marking — not a flag'. Flipping it silently would be the exact failure the three-state/provenance discipline exists to prevent.
- There is no output schema for a 2D semantic region anywhere in the repo: AnnotationRecord is a 3D box and GroupAnnotation is declared-but-unproduced. A road stage must either define a new record dataclass in pipeline/common/schemas.py with its own validate(), or terminate at the mask tree + a CVAT export, and say in its manifest which of the two it is (§4 capability-gap discipline).

### Blockers

- Reusing Stage 4 with a synthetic full-image box is NOT viable as a shortcut, for a hard reason and not a stylistic one: the synthetic box would have to be appended to Stage 3's `boxes_xyxy_px` array (that is the only thing Stage 4 iterates, masks.py:1870), which changes n_boxes and therefore shifts nothing but ADDS an index that Stage 5 will then treat as a real instance — build_instances (lift.py:281-293) filters only on `kept`, not on class, so the road region would be lifted, painted, clustered by DBSCAN against a missing prior, tracked, inflated and gated as an object. Suppressing it would require a class-based filter in Stage 5, Stage 6, Stage 7, Stage 8, Stage 9 and two exporters. A separate stage is genuinely cleaner.
- There is no test file for Stage 4: `ls tests/` shows only test_armb_configs.py, test_export_armb.py, test_export_release.py, test_stage3c_check.py, test_stage3_merge.py. The count/order/resolution contract is enforced only by in-code asserts, so any change to the mask representation has no regression net — a new stage cannot lean on an existing Stage 4 test suite for its own contract.
- Stage 4's load_upstream does not pass current_fingerprint to require_upstream (masks.py:1805-1810), so Stage 4 alone would accept a Stage 3 tree built against a different dataroot; it only inherits and re-stamps the fingerprint. A new road stage should not copy this omission — Stage 7 (track.py:1464-1470) shows the checked form.
- MaskResult's validator is box-count-shaped ('one mask per box, in order', model_interfaces.py:362-363). Any stuff payload passed through it must either carry n_boxes=1 (a semantic lie) or get a new payload dataclass. Reusing MaskResult unchanged for N-class stuff maps would silently pass validation while meaning something different.
- The `provider` field and the whole checkpoint-attribution apparatus assume a hub id or a local weights file plus a pinned sha256 (masks.py:2024-2033, :1052-1082, :2196-2201). A road model that ships as a torchvision/mmseg checkpoint or a config+weights pair has no place in CheckpointSpec as used here without extending it; spec.validate() already refuses a missing revision (masks.py:2030-2035).

### Open questions

- Is the road stage meant to be prompt-driven (SAM 3.1 accepts text/concept prompts through the same multiplex predictor, and the adapter's own comments at masks.py:1497-1500 say the `bounding_boxes=`/exemplar spelling 'routes to the semantic exemplar path' which calls reset_state) or a dedicated semantic-segmentation model? I did not exercise or load any model, so I cannot say whether the installed sam3 package's semantic path returns a usable road mask, only that the current adapter deliberately avoids it.
- Does the road stage output need to reach the 3D chain at all (a BEV drivable surface for Stage 9's locked-off drivable_term), or does it terminate at 2D masks + CVAT export? The answer changes whether a new record type in pipeline/common/schemas.py is needed and whether §11 decision 7 has to be reopened. I found the hook but no decision record enabling it.
- Front-camera-only or all 8 cameras? Nothing in the repo states a policy for a road stage; the cost difference measured against Stage 4's throughput is roughly 8x (~10 min vs ~60 min per pilot run).
- Whether two GPU stages may run concurrently: apply_vram_cap sets torch.cuda.set_per_process_memory_fraction per process (model_interfaces.py:1266), which does not coordinate between processes. I could not find any lock other than run_stages.sh's whole-run .run_stages.lock (observed at /home/mt/dhakascenes/work/.run_stages.lock), so whether concurrent GPU stages are permitted is a policy question I cannot answer from the code.
- What the C-decision register (C1, C16, C19, C26, C27, §11 decision 7, comprehensive.md §1.4/§7.3.4) actually says — the code cites these constantly but I did not locate the decision documents themselves in the repo, so I could not verify the quoted rationales beyond the docstrings that cite them.
- Whether the six-camera camera_priority list (missing CAM_LEFT/CAM_RIGHT) is deliberate or a latent defect: those two channels sort to the sentinel 99 in Stage 4's NMS tie-break and to len(priority) in Stage 5's instance ordering. It is deterministic either way, so it is not a correctness bug, but a road stage that copies camera_priority should know it is incomplete for this 8-camera rig.
- Whether a road region should participate in any cross-camera reconciliation at all (e.g. agreeing that the road seen by CAM_FRONT and CAM_FRONT_LEFT is one surface). Stage 4's angular-footprint machinery could express it, but the IoA-of-a-tight-box formulation is wrong for a non-convex stuff region and I have no evidence about what the design wants here.

## 4. Scout — Downstream of Stage 4: stage5_lift, stage6_cluster, stage7_track, stage8_inflate, stage9_qa, plus the run_stages.sh wiring that drives them

**Summary.** Everything downstream of Stage 4 is a strictly linear, instance-keyed chain: Stage 5 turns each KEPT Stage-4 mask into a keyframe-local integer `instance_id` and paints LiDAR point indices into it; Stage 6 DBSCANs each instance's points and fits one oriented box per instance; Stage 7 assigns `track_id` by class-gated predict-then-match over those boxes; Stage 8 inflates each box toward a class prior; Stage 9 turns each row into an `AnnotationRecord` pre-label. The join key is `(keyframe_token, channel, proposal_index)` plus the keyframe-local `instance_id`, and every stage re-emits the upstream row verbatim and adds fields, so the row schema is strictly cumulative from stage6/boxes.jsonl -> stage7/boxes.jsonl -> stage8/inflated.jsonl. Each stage takes explicit `--<name>-dir` arguments defaulting to `<work_root>/<stage_name>`, gates every upstream through `require_upstream()` (manifest + marker + metadata fingerprint + degraded opt-in), enumerates work from the upstream's `scenes/` directory, and nothing anywhere enumerates `work_root` itself — so a new sibling stage directory is completely invisible to the existing chain. The chain assumes instances are compact, boxable, per-camera-mask objects with a class name that has a prior and a taxonomy phrase; a non-instance road region injected inline would not crash Stage 5-8 (it degrades quietly into a fallback-epsilon L-shape box) but would corrupt Stage 6/8 statistics, pollute eval_3d precision silently, and hard-crash `export_cvat_3d.py` on a `label_id[class_name]` KeyError. Stage 9 exists but is not a step in `scripts/run_stages.sh` at all and has never run on this substrate.

### Implications

- A road-segmentation stage MUST NOT inject a road mask as an extra Stage 4 candidate row. If it appends a mask with a matching kept candidate, Stage 5 lifts it as a normal instance (lift.py:281), Stage 6 fits an L-shape box for it with a fallback epsilon and does not refuse (cluster.py:158, priors.py:270), Stage 7 tracks it, Stage 8 leaves it uninflated as no_prior_for_class, Stage 9 emits it as a pre-label with an unvalidated category — and then export_cvat_3d.py:203 crashes the chain on KeyError while eval_3d.py:162 silently counts it as false positives.
- If a road mask is inserted anywhere that shifts per-channel mask indices, Stage 5 refuses loudly at lift.py:466-471 (proposal_index >= n_masks). That is the good failure mode, but it means an inline design is a one-line-away difference between a loud refusal and a silent corruption — an argument against touching the mask npz at all.
- A road stage cannot get road-surface LiDAR points from any Stage 1 output: Stage 1 deletes the ground band (ingest.py:723) and writes only the survivors (ingest.py:740-745). It must read the raw blob via keyframes.jsonl's `lidar_path` in the read-only dataroot, or reuse Stage 1's already-persisted per-keyframe `sector_planes` from filter_diagnostics.json.
- A road region has no place in the existing record vocabulary: AnnotationRecord requires strictly-positive size on all three axes and a quaternion (schemas.py:893-895), and Stage 9 keys pre-label tokens on (keyframe, channel, proposal_index) with a uniqueness refusal (gate.py:448-452). A road product needs its own record type + RECORD_TYPES entry (schemas.py:979-985), or its own non-schemas artifact format.
- Adding a road class to the priors file is a chain-wide change, not a local one: the sha256 is checked by Stage 8 against Stage 6's manifest (inflate.py:782-790) and by Stage 9 against Stage 8's (gate.py:359-365), so changing priors forces 6/8/9 to be re-run together or they refuse.
- A parallel side-branch stage is genuinely free: nothing enumerates work_root, each stage names its upstream directories explicitly, boxes_dir() only looks at stage7_track/stage6_cluster, and select_stage3_dir_for_4() only looks at stage3* — so a new <work_root>/stage4r_road tree cannot be picked up by any existing consumer by accident.
- The new stage must implement the full discipline itself, not inherit it: clear_markers() before the first byte, require_upstream() on every upstream with current_fingerprint and accept_degraded, a run_manifest.json recording upstream.metadata_fingerprint + per-upstream degraded flags + accepted_degraded_upstream, and write_marker() with three states. run_step's stage-dir severity mode cross-examines rc against the marker (run_stages.sh:611-645) and will abort the chain if the two disagree.
- Whatever the road stage consumes from Stage 1 will meet a DEGRADED marker on this substrate today, so it must accept --accept-degraded-upstream from the wrapper exactly as stages 1 and 3-8 do (run_stages.sh:563-566), and record it.
- Wiring a new step means editing FOUR places in run_stages.sh (the argument case at :262, the dispatch case inside the loop at :664-921, the OPT_IN_STEPS list at :240, and the markers summary list at :936) plus run_pilot.py (STAGE_DIRS at :106, CHAIN at :120, stage_cmd at :190-230) — otherwise the two orchestrators disagree the way they already do about step 9.
- Adding the step to ALL_STEPS would change what a default run means and would insert a GPU-resident model into the default chain; every prior opt-in arm (3b, 3f, 3m, 3c) deliberately stayed out of ALL_STEPS for exactly this reason (run_stages.sh:78-80, 235-240).
- Do not re-open Stage 9's `drivable_term`: GateConfig.validate refuses any value but 'off' (gate.py:192-196), and that refusal is a locked §11 decision-7 record, not a flag. A road stage that wants to feed the QA gate is a spec change, not an integration.
- Non-instance road output flowing through Stage 6/8 would also quietly corrupt their reported quality numbers even when nothing crashes: n_instances, boxes_per_keyframe, ghost_fraction, yaw_ambiguous_fraction (cluster.py:1172-1184), n_eps_fallback / eps_fallback_classes, and Stage 8's triggered_fraction / inflated_fraction / n_no_prior (inflate.py:941-948) all use the same denominators as the object chain.

## 5. Scout — QA, metrics and evaluation — what quality is measured today, and what ground truth exists for a road-surface segmentation stage

**Summary.** Quality in this pipeline is measured in two disconnected places. (1) Stage 9 (`pipeline/stage9_qa/gate.py`) is a per-box GATE, not a metric: it turns confidence + LiDAR-return count + a 2x-class-prior size check into a tier (auto_accept / flagged / rejected). Its drivable-area term is hard-locked OFF and `GateConfig.validate()` refuses any other value, so "is this box on the road?" is currently an unevaluated `None` by design. Stage 9 is not wired into `scripts/run_stages.sh` at all — it is only reachable through `scripts/run_pilot.py --steps 9`. (2) The `eval` step runs four diagnostic scripts (`export_gt_coco.py`, `paint_metrics.py`, `eval_2d.py`, `eval_3d.py`), all `soft` severity: they write no markers, nothing consumes them, and a crash or an all-zero result is recorded as `warn`/`ok` and the chain continues. Every one of those metrics scores against the dataset's own human labels. The Dhaka pilot has none: `sample_annotation.json` is literally `[]` (3 bytes), as are `category`, `instance`, `attribute` and `visibility`; there is no `maps/`, no lidarseg, no panoptic, no semantic annotation of any kind. The metrics on disk prove it — `detect2d_metrics.json` reports 45,936 predictions against 0 GT boxes and 0.0 precision/recall everywhere, and `detect3d_metrics.json` reports 23,627 predicted boxes against 0 eligible GT. `paint_metrics.py`, the document-declared "single strongest correctness signal", crashed on this run and wrote no file. The only human-corrected data anywhere (`cvat_export_fixed/`, `review_sam31/fixes.jsonl`) is on the OLD nuScenes-mini scenes (scene-0061/0103/0553), never on `chunk_0000`. Consequently there is today no measurement of Dhaka output quality of any kind, and a road-segmentation stage would inherit exactly that vacuum.

### Implications

- A road-segmentation stage CANNOT report an accuracy number on the Dhaka pilot today. There is no drivable-area GT, no lidarseg, no map expansion, no human labels, and no held-out split. Any headline metric it emits would be a self-consistency number, and the design must say so on the artifact rather than let a reader read it as accuracy.
- It must NOT copy the eval_2d/eval_3d failure mode now live on disk: those scripts return 0 and print `0.0%` against a zero-size GT denominator, and run_stages.sh records them as `ok`. A road-seg metric must distinguish 'measured zero' from 'nothing to measure against' — refuse (exit 2) or emit an explicit `gt_available: false` / null, never 0.0. compare_runs.py already encodes this principle ('A cell that is missing a metric prints `--`, never 0.0') and is the precedent to follow.
- has_ground_truth() only inspects sample_annotation.json, so any road GT stored elsewhere is invisible to it. The stage needs its OWN substrate probe (e.g. 'is there a road_gt export for these scenes?'), and it must not try to satisfy the existing probe by writing into the dataroot — dataroot is read-only by path contract (configs/paths.yaml:7-9).
- The stage must write run_manifest.json plus exactly one of _SUCCESS / _SUCCESS.degraded (with causes), bind the metadata fingerprint, consume upstream via require_upstream(), and use 0/1/2 exit semantics. run_stages.sh's run_step verifies the marker on disk rather than trusting the exit code, so a stage that exits 1 without writing a degraded marker will abort the chain.
- It should enter run_stages.sh as an OPT_IN step (the 3b/3f/3m/3c precedent at run_stages.sh:240), not appended to ALL_STEPS: 'a default run must keep meaning what it meant before the stage existed' (run_pilot.py:117-119). It must also be added to the --clean-slate purge list (run_stages.sh:360-366) or a stale road-seg tree will sit beside a fresh run.
- There is no schema record for a semantic mask. AnnotationRecord is cuboid-only and Stage 4's npz is instance-indexed by proposal position. A road-surface output needs a NEW versioned contract and its own writer; it must not be smuggled into AnnotationRecord (schemas validate() would reject it, and 'category' + cuboid geometry would misdescribe a surface).
- The QA gate's drivable term is a LOCKED decision (§11 decision 7) that validate() actively refuses. If the design wants road segmentation to feed spatial_ok, that is a deliberate reversal of a locked decision requiring the no_production_counterpart marking the code names — not a config flag. The safer path is to leave GateVector untouched and publish road output on its own contract.
- The existing LiDAR ground-plane machinery is the natural geometric cross-check, and it is already broken on this substrate: 76.4% of sector fits are rejected and substituted with a global plane, which is the sole cause of Stage 1's DEGRADED marker. Any 'LiDAR agreement' proxy must read the per-sector `fallback` / `rejected_fit` flags and exclude fallback sectors, or it will silently compare the camera mask against a single flat plane fitted to the whole cloud.
- Ground points are removed from both persisted clouds; only counts remain. A per-point ground/non-ground label is recomputable (single_sweep cloud + sector_planes + ground_band_m 0.3 via ingest.ground_distance) but is not on disk today — the design should decide whether Stage 1 must additionally persist the ground-point subset, which changes a stage that is currently frozen and degraded.
- Any road-area, coverage or density statistic must go through pipeline/common/eval_region.in_region(); E is currently R2 with a 30 m cap and the file states that runs across the 40->30 m change are not comparable. A locally reimplemented radius is exactly the failure that file exists to prevent.
- Human review infrastructure already supports masks: export_cvat_coco emits cv2-contour polygons, cvat_setup imports COCO 1.0, and review_fix_sam31 serves a SAM 3.1 click-to-segment page. A road-surface GT project can be built on the existing C13 pattern (pipeline output in one CVAT project, human answer key in a separate project, never --replace on the answer key).
- nuScenes v1.0-mini with drivable_area/road_segment/lane/walkway polygons is on disk and gives a real, zero-human-cost road-surface GT — but for Boston/Singapore. It is usable as a CODE-CORRECTNESS regression harness for the projection/segmentation math (the same role Results/COMPARISON.md plays for detection), and must never be reported as a Dhaka quality claim.
- paint_metrics.py currently crashes and hardcodes configs/taxonomy_pilot_nuscenes.yaml. If a road-seg design plans to sit beside or extend it, that script needs repair and substrate-parameterisation first; today the pipeline's declared strongest correctness signal produces no file at all.
- There are zero tests over any QA/metric code and tests/unit does not exist. A new metric added here would ship untested by the repo's current practice — the design should carry its own test file under tests/ (the test_stage3c_check.py precedent) or the pipeline's 'refuse rather than emit silently-wrong output' discipline does not extend to it.

## 6. Scout — Publication, export and visualization — how DhakaScenes annotations reach humans (COCO/CVAT 2D, Datumaro 3D, PNG renders)

**Summary.** Annotations reach humans through three independent, marker-free tails hanging off the marker-disciplined stage chain. (1) `scripts/export_cvat_coco.py` folds Stage 3 boxes + Stage 4 masks into ONE COCO 1.0 `instances.json` per scene under `work_root/cvat_export/<scene>/`, writing TWO annotations per kept object — a rectangle carrying the detector's box claim with empty `segmentation`, and a polygon twin carrying the SAM mask as cv2 `RETR_EXTERNAL` contours — plus bbox-only rows for Stage-4-suppressed duplicates; the COCO `categories` table is built by sorting the taxonomy YAML's `prompt_phrase` VALUES and numbering them 1..N, and every annotation indexes it with `category_id[c["class_name"]]`, which is the KeyError. (2) `scripts/cvat_setup.py` creates one CVAT project per provenance level (machine vs. answer key), writes the label schema ONCE at project creation from the export's own `categories` plus a five-attribute contract, creates one task per scene FROM THE SHARE volume (no image bytes copied), and imports the JSON as format `"COCO 1.0"`. ### Implications

- ANSWER TO THE KEY QUESTION — OPTION A (recommended default): add the road as an extra COCO annotation in the SAME instances.json, with `segmentation` as COCO RLE (`{"counts": ..., "size": [720, 1280]}`) and `iscrowd: 1`, and a new taxonomy phrase (e.g. `a road surface`) so it gets a real category id. CVAT's COCO 1.0 importer accepts Masks and turns them into native editable MASK shapes (bindings.py:2411, 2485-2486), and COCO is the ONLY import format here that also preserves per-annotation attributes (format-coco.md), which the pipeline's provenance discipline requires. Cost: it doubles down on the already-102 MB single JSON; an RLE for a road covering ~40% of 1280x720 is a few KB compressed but 18,416 of them is real weight. Requires: `mask_to_polygons` is NOT reusable — a new RLE encoder is needed; and `iscrowd: 1` must be verified against `_REQUIRE_ISCROWD = False` (coco.py:26).
- ANSWER — OPTION B (only if you accept polygons): reuse `mask_to_polygons()` verbatim and emit the road as a polygon annotation in the same file. Zero new encoding code, and it is the shape the existing project schema and reviewer workflow already handle (vertex dragging). Cost: `RETR_EXTERNAL` fills holes, so a traffic island or a car sitting on the road becomes road; `approxPolyDP(epsilon=1.0)` plus the 20 px floor discards thin road slivers; and a road boundary is far more vertex-hungry than an object outline, so file size and CVAT canvas performance both degrade. A road polygon is also the hardest shape to CORRECT by vertex dragging, which is the point of publishing it.
- ANSWER — OPTION C (separate project, raster masks): a SECOND CVAT project + task set importing 'Cityscapes 1.0' or 'Segmentation mask 1.1' zips of full-image indexed PNGs. This is the only route that publishes a true full-image semantic label map (background included) and it matches Cityscapes' own `road`/`sidewalk`/`terrain` vocabulary. Cost, all verified: (i) both importers run `masks_to_polygons` unless `conv_mask_to_poly=False` is threaded through `cvat_setup.py`'s `task.import_annotations()` call, which today passes neither kwarg; (ii) 'Attributes: Not supported' on both — every `score`/`source`/`hops` provenance value the pipeline is built to carry is LOST, which is exactly the silent-loss failure `undeclared_attributes()` exists to refuse; (iii) it needs a colour/index contract between the exporter and the CVAT project labels (`make_colormap` inserts `background` #000000; Cityscapes synthesizes label_color.txt, VOC-style does not); (iv) it duplicates the 18,416-frame task, and 2D tasks come from the SHARE volume, so the same images are re-registered, not re-uploaded — cheap in bytes, expensive in reviewer navigation.
- A road class MUST be added to the taxonomy YAML's `prompt_phrase` values, not injected at export time, or `category_id[c["class_name"]]` (export_cvat_coco.py:117,140) KeyErrors — the same C28 failure the just-added `--taxonomy` flag was written for. Adding it also changes `sorted(set(...))`, which RENUMBERS every existing category id; `eval_2d.py` survives that because it resolves ids to names per document (eval_2d.py:226-227), but any consumer that assumes stable ids will not.

A road phrase must therefore be APPENDED, or live in a fourth taxonomy file that the road stage owns and the detection exporters do not read.
- If the road annotation carries a NON-EMPTY `segmentation`, `eval_2d.py:123-125` drops it before matching, so detection metrics are unharmed. If it is ever emitted with an EMPTY `segmentation` (a bbox-only road row, or a road polygon whose contours all fall under the 20 px floor) it becomes a counted prediction and corrupts precision. A road stage that shares `instances.json` must guarantee its rows always carry a non-empty segmentation, or `predictions_of()` must gain an explicit class-based exclusion.

## 7. Scout — Model landscape for road / drivable-surface segmentation — external model/weights/licence research plus an audit of what is already cached and runnable on this box

**Summary.** There is no road-segmentation subsystem today; what exists is a closed four-role model registry (`embedding_ood`, `proposal_2d`, `mask_2d`, `reid_embedding`) whose adapters all load a pinned checkpoint, apply the C1 synthetic VRAM cap before the first allocation, and record checkpoint id/revision/sha256 plus a `vram_cap` block into `run_manifest.json`. Every model in the chain today is object-centric: Stage 3 runs YOLO11x over a 10-phrase object caption, Stage 3b/4 run SAM 3 / SAM 3.1 as box-prompted segmenters, and Stage 3c runs a local Nemotron VLM through llama.cpp that refuses to start if any other process holds the GPU. The substrate is 8 cameras x 2302 keyframes = 18,416 images at 1280x720, and Stage 4 segments all of them in 3561 s at a measured 7456 MiB peak — so a road stage has both a proven throughput envelope and roughly 14 GB of unused VRAM headroom under the 22000 MiB cap the last run used. The single most important external finding is that `facebook/sam3` — already downloaded here, licence already granted, 859.9 M params, and text-promptable through the installed `transformers` 5.15.0 — scores 65.2 zero-shot mIoU on Cityscapes when prompted one noun phrase at a time, versus 44.2 for the strongest specialist baseline, while class-agnostic SAM 1 fragments road surfaces at 0.13–0.17 mIoU. The second is a licence cliff: SegFormer's NVIDIA weights, all Mapillary-Vistas-derived weights, all Cityscapes-derived weights and the whole YOLOP/BDD100K family are research/non-commercial, whereas SAM 3's licence explicitly permits commercial use — so the choice of segmenter is as much a licence decision as a quality one.

### Implications

- RANKED RECOMMENDATION #1 — facebook/sam3 with the text prompt 'road' (or 'road surface'), as a NEW role reusing the existing adapter pattern. It is the only candidate that is (a) already downloaded and licence-granted, (b) commercially licensable under the SAM License, (c) already measured on this box at 2.1 GiB / ~0.7 s, and (d) backed by the strongest published evidence for the exact task: 65.2 zero-shot mIoU on Cityscapes versus 44.2 for APE, prompted one noun phrase at a time. Cost of adoption is close to zero: no download, no new dependency, and Sam3Processor already takes `text=`. The honest tradeoff is that 65.2 mIoU is a proposal, not a label — which is exactly what an annotation pipeline that ends in CVAT human review wants — and that SAM 3's own scope statement is about noun phrases denoting objects, so 'road' is an off-label use that MUST be smoke-tested before it is designed around.
- RANKED RECOMMENDATION #2 — facebook/mask2former-swin-large-mapillary-vistas-semantic as the SEMANTICS arm, not the geometry arm. It is the only off-the-shelf checkpoint whose taxonomy already names Pothole, Curb, Curb Cut, Crosswalk-Plain, Lane Marking, Service Lane, Manhole, Catch Basin and Rail Track — i.e. the road-surface vocabulary Dhaka actually needs — and Mapillary is the most geographically diverse street-level training set (six continents including Asia) with published evidence that it beats Cityscapes on exactly the ground classes at issue. 216 M params comfortably fits the budget. The blocking tradeoff is licence: Mapillary Vistas is CC BY-NC-SA, so these weights are research-only. Use it as a second opinion / class-refinement pass over SAM 3's region, and record the licence inheritance in the manifest the way C28 already records it.
- RANKED RECOMMENDATION #3 — tue-mps/cityscapes_semantic_eomt_large_1024 or tue-mps/eomt-dinov3-ade-semantic-large-512 as the MIT-licensed fallback. EoMT is CVPR 2025, encoder-only (no heavy pixel decoder), ~0.3 B params, MIT-tagged, actively maintained (Jan 2026 DINOv3 refresh), and already importable from the installed transformers 5.15.0. Pick the ADE variant if licence cleanliness matters more than driving-scene fit; pick the Cityscapes variant if the reverse. Caveat to carry: the DINOv3 variants depend on Meta's separately gated DINOv3 License for the backbone, so the MIT tag alone does not clear them.
- RANKED RECOMMENDATION #4 — TwinLiteNet / YOLOPv2 as a cheap sanity baseline, NOT as the primary. At 0.4 M params and 415 FPS they would process all 18,416 images in under a minute and give a free second opinion on drivable area. But their 91-93% BDD100K numbers are on structured US highways with painted lanes; a model whose entire notion of 'drivable' was learned from BDD100K is the single worst prior for unmarked, unpaved, rickshaw-choked Dhaka streets. Their weights also inherit BDD100K's commercial restriction despite MIT code. Use them to detect disagreement, never to arbitrate it.
- DO NOT use class-agnostic SAM (SAM 1 / MobileSAM 'segment everything', or SAM 3 without a concept prompt) for the road region. There is direct published evidence it fails at 0.13-0.17 mIoU on road scenes and specifically fragments the road surface into multiple masks when asphalt varies — which is the Dhaka default, not the exception. The locally cached mobile_sam.pt is not a road-segmentation fallback.
- The licence question is upstream of the model question and must be settled first. If DhakaScenes labels are ever commercial, SegFormer (all nvidia/segformer-*, and by inheritance both izzako IDD checkpoints), every Mapillary-Vistas-derived checkpoint, every Cityscapes-derived checkpoint and the whole BDD100K-trained YOLOP family are all out. What survives: facebook/sam3 (SAM License permits commercial), ADE20K/COCO-trained EoMT (MIT), and nvidia/NV-OneFormer (NVIDIA Open Model Agreement, commercially-licensed training images). That collapses the candidate set to essentially recommendation #1 plus #3.
- A road stage must add a fifth role to pipeline/common/model_interfaces.py:121 (e.g. `surface_2d`) with matching ROLE_PROTOCOLS and ROLE_METHODS entries, or explicitly reuse `mask_2d`. The registry raises RegistryError on any role not in the closed tuple, so this is not optional and cannot be worked around by convention.
- It must call apply_vram_cap(torch, device) BEFORE its first allocation and emit the {value_mib, enforced, physical_device_mib, device_name} block into its run_manifest.json, exactly as Stage 3/3b/4 do — a cap applied after the model loads caps nothing (model_interfaces.py:1232 docstring).
- It must write the three-state marker (_SUCCESS / _SUCCESS.degraded with causes / none), call clear_markers() before its first byte, consume upstream through require_upstream(), and exit 2 on refusal. A road stage that emits a plausible-but-wrong full-image 'road' mask silently is precisely the failure the marker discipline exists to prevent — so 'road region covers >X% or <Y% of the frame' is a natural degraded-marker cause.
- It must run at 1280x720 native and record image_size_px, and any internal resize must preserve aspect the way proposals.py:1077 asserts. This directly conflicts with SAM 3's processor, which forces a 1008x1008 SQUARE at a 288x288 mask size — the design must decide explicitly whether to letterbox or accept the distortion, and record which.
- If it publishes to CVAT through scripts/export_cvat_coco.py, mask_to_polygons at :41 uses cv2.RETR_EXTERNAL and will silently FILL the vehicle-shaped holes in a road mask — turning 'road visible under this rickshaw' into 'road includes this rickshaw'. Either extend that helper to RETR_CCOMP with hole handling, emit COCO RLE instead of polygons, or route the road stage through a separate export. This is a concrete breakage, not a stylistic preference.
- The class space must not be widened silently. 'a road barrier' is already a live phrase in the 10-phrase caption; adding a road-surface phrase changes the caption bytes and therefore the caption_sha256 that Stage 4's manifest binds to (stage4 upstream.prompt_caption_sha256), and per C28 widening the vocabulary is the merge's job, not a stage's. A road stage is safest emitting into its own namespace with its own caption rather than joining the object caption.
- Stage 1's per-sector RANSAC ground plane is an independent, already-computed estimate of the same physical surface and is the obvious cross-check for a road mask (project the mask, compare against the ground inliers). But it was DEGRADED on the last run at rejection_rate 0.764, so the design must treat it as corroboration, not authority — and must not inherit a hard refusal from it.
- GPU exclusivity: if the road stage loads a big model it should adopt the assert_gpu_exclusive() pattern from stage3c_check/check.py:291 rather than trusting the sequential driver, and it must never share a chain position with Stage 3c (the Nemotron GGUF alone is 23.9 GB and only fits via CPU MoE offload).
- The Nemotron VLM can assist but cannot segment. VLMs emit text, not masks. The correct use, and it maps one-to-one onto the proven Stage 3c pattern, is to take an already-produced road polygon, crop it, and ask the VLM BLIND for the SURFACE CLASS — paved / unpaved / damaged / waterlogged / under-construction — recording a per-polygon verdict in {confirmed, relabeled, unclear, skipped_small, error}. That is exactly the axis where the geographic-bias paper says the models fail (classification, 10-90% of segmentation bias) and where localization transfers fine. It is also the only way to get 'drivable fallback' semantics without IDD weights.
- Throughput is a non-issue at this scale and should not drive the choice. Even the slowest candidate at the measured SAM-3 rate of 0.7 s/image finishes CAM_FRONT (2,302 images) in ~27 minutes and all eight cameras (18,416) in ~3.6 hours — against Stage 4's existing 59-minute and Stage 3b's 102-minute precedents. Optimise for label quality and licence, not FPS.

### Blockers

- LICENCE CLIFF, unresolved and upstream of every model decision: SegFormer's NVIDIA weights are explicitly non-commercial ('may be used or intended for use non-commercially ... for research or evaluation purposes only'), Mapillary Vistas is CC BY-NC-SA, Cityscapes is non-commercial, and BDD100K requires a UC Berkeley licence for commercial use. That eliminates SegFormer (incl. both IDD checkpoints, which derive from nvidia/mit-b5), the Mapillary Mask2Former, the Cityscapes Mask2Former/OneFormer/EoMT, and the whole YOLOP/YOLOPv2/HybridNets/TwinLiteNet family — unless DhakaScenes is a research-only artefact. Nobody has told me which it is.
- NO public checkpoint exists with the label a Dhaka road stage most needs. IDD is the only taxonomy that names 'drivable fallback' / 'non-drivable fallback' (the unpaved shoulder that is in practice drivable), and the only two IDD checkpoints on the Hub — izzako/segformer-b4- and -b5-finetuned-IDD-L2_v2 — collapse to a 16-class level-2 set that does NOT include either fallback class. Getting that label means training locally, which needs IDD access (rights reserved by the IDD team) and a training stage this pipeline does not have.
- ZERO measured VRAM or throughput on this 4090 for any non-SAM segmentation candidate. Every number I can give for SegFormer, Mask2Former, OneFormer or EoMT is a parameter count or a secondary-source figure on other hardware. The only measured anchors are the project's own: SAM 3 = 2.1 GiB / ~0.7 s, SAM 3.1 multiplex = 7,456 MiB peak, llmdet_large = 7.5 GiB. A design that commits to Mask2Former Swin-L on a VRAM estimate is committing on a guess.
- SAM 3's road capability is INFERRED, not demonstrated on this substrate. The Cityscapes 65.2 mIoU is on a German structured-road benchmark and the paper reports no per-class breakdown I could reach; SAM 3's own scope sentence restricts concepts to noun phrases denoting objects. Whether 'road' returns one coherent region or fragments on cracked, patched, waterlogged Dhaka asphalt is unknown and I was correctly forbidden from testing it.
- BRSSD10K — the one Bangladesh-specific road SEGMENTATION dataset I found (10,082 images, nine cities including Dhaka) — could not be verified. Both OpenReview URLs returned a bot-verification page rather than the paper, so its class list, availability, licence and baseline numbers are search-result hearsay and I have not asserted them as facts.
- Three concrete code contracts block a road stage from shipping regardless of model choice: ROLES is a closed 4-tuple (model_interfaces.py:121), run_stages.sh's step list is a closed shell case that exits 2 on anything else (:262, :275), and export_cvat_coco.py's RETR_EXTERNAL polygon path (:43) silently fills mask holes — which for a road mask means every occluding vehicle becomes road.

### Open questions

- Is DhakaScenes a research artefact or a commercial dataset? This single answer removes roughly two-thirds of the candidate list. It should be settled before any weights are downloaded.
- Does facebook/sam3 with text='road' return ONE coherent region on Dhaka frames, or does it fragment the way class-agnostic SAM 1 does (published 0.13-0.17 mIoU, 'over-segmentation of road areas into multiple distinct masks')? Cheap decisive experiment, forbidden in this session: run the cached SAM 3 on ~50 CAM_FRONT frames from chunk_0000, count connected components per frame, record peak VRAM. That is one afternoon and it decides the whole design.
- What is the right noun phrase? SAM 3 is documented as sensitive to phrasing and to 'out-of-domain terms'. Candidates worth A/B-ing on the same 50 frames: 'road', 'road surface', 'the road', 'drivable area', 'paved road', 'street'. The pipeline already has a precedent for recording caption provenance and a caption_sha256, so this is testable inside the existing discipline.
- Does SAM 3 return the VISIBLE road (modal) or the inferred road behind vehicles (amodal)? Dhaka frames are heavily occluded by rickshaws and CNGs; the answer determines whether the stage's output is a free-space mask or a surface-extent mask, and those are different annotation products with different downstream contracts in Stage 5's lift.
- Do Mapillary Vistas' Pothole / Curb / Lane Marking / Service Lane classes survive transfer to Dhaka, or do they fire on shadows, water and debris? Unmeasured. Worth checking on the same 50 frames before committing to that checkpoint as the semantics arm.
- How much South Asian imagery is actually in Mapillary Vistas? The paper says six continents including Asia, but I found no country-level breakdown. If Bangladesh/India coverage is near zero, the 'globally diverse' argument for those weights is much weaker than it sounds.
- Does the team already hold IDD access (rights are reserved by the IDD team and require agreeing to their terms)? If yes, fine-tuning EoMT or Mask2Former on IDD level-3 — the only label set with 'drivable fallback' — becomes viable and the whole ranking changes.
- What resolution should the stage run at, and does it letterbox? Stage 3 pins 1280x720 native and asserts aspect preservation on its internal 1600x928 resize; SAM 3's processor forces 1008x1008 square at a 288x288 mask size. The design must choose and record which distortion it accepts, because road occupies a wide shallow band where vertical squeeze matters most.
- Should the road stage be per-frame or temporal? SAM 3 / SAM 3.1 support video propagation and Stage 3b already runs that machinery; road is the most temporally stable thing in the scene, so propagating one prompt across a scene could be both cheaper and more consistent than 2,302 independent segmentations. Nobody has measured whether propagation drifts on a surface class.
- Can the Nemotron VLM classify road-surface TYPE reliably enough to be worth a stage? The Stage 3c pattern is proven for object crops at >=32 px, but a road polygon is a large irregular region, not a crop, and no smoke test exists for surface-type questions ('is this paved, unpaved, waterlogged, or under construction?').
- Which class-space does the road output join? A separate namespace avoids the C28 vocabulary-authority machinery entirely; joining the object caption changes caption_sha256 and drags Stage 4's upstream binding with it. This is a contract decision, not an implementation detail.
- Should the road mask be cross-checked against Stage 1's per-sector RANSAC ground plane, and what happens on disagreement? The ground fit was degraded at rejection_rate 0.764 on the last run, so it cannot gate the road stage — but a recorded agreement statistic between the two independent estimates would be a genuinely strong QA signal that nothing else in the pipeline provides.

## 8. Synthesis


### Map

#### What a road stage must fit into

**Shape of the pipeline.** Stage 0 probes, Stage 1 ingests (the only place ground geometry is computed), Stage 3/3b/3f/3m/3c produce 2D object proposals, Stage 4 segments them, Stages 5→6→7→8→9 lift/cluster/track/inflate/gate them into 3D `AnnotationRecord` pre-labels. Three markerless tails hang off it: `eval` (metrics), `viz` (PNGs), `cvat`/`cvat3d` (publish). The chain from Stage 4 onward is strictly instance-keyed on `(keyframe_token, channel, proposal_index)` plus a keyframe-local `instance_id`, and each stage re-emits its upstream row verbatim and adds fields.

**Substrate.** One scene `chunk_0000`, 2302 keyframes, 8 ring cameras = 18,416 images at **1280×720** (verified: `schemas.py:120-121`; the "1600×900" in `lift.py`'s MaskFile docstring is stale prose — scouts disagreed, the constants win). LiDAR single-sweep clouds are a fusion, 79.6% ZED stereo (ring 10/11) and 20.4% Livox (ring 0-3). Verified marker state today: stage0/1/3/3b **degraded**, stages 4-8 clean. Any new consumer of Stage 1 must pass `--accept-degraded-upstream` and record it.

**The critical geometry finding.** Stage 1 fits and persists 8 azimuthal sector planes per keyframe (18,416 total, in one 23 MB `filter_diagnostics.json`) — but on this substrate **they are not the road**. `LIDAR_TOP`'s `calibrated_sensor` is identity, so ego z=0 is the sensor, while the road sits at z ≈ −2.15 m. The RANSAC candidate band z∈[−1.5,+1.5] (whose provenance note assumes "z=0 at ground") excludes the road entirely; all persisted planes have d ∈ [−0.72, 0.0], median −0.228; 76.4% of sector fits were rejected and replaced by a global plane — which is precisely why Stage 1's marker is degraded. Two scouts described `sector_planes` as "the closest thing to a road-surface signal"; a third measured it and showed it sits ~1.9 m above the actual road. **The measurement wins.** The silver lining: because the plane is too high, the road surface *survives* ground removal — 52.1% of kept single-sweep points on a sampled keyframe lie in the real road band. The road data is on disk and correct; only the plane is wrong.

**Where a road stage cannot go.**
- *Not through Stage 4's tree.* The npz array index **is** the Stage 3 proposal index; Stage 5, Stage 7, the CVAT exporter and the renderer all read `candidates[].proposal_index` as that index. Injecting a synthetic full-image box would make Stage 5 lift the road as a real instance (`build_instances` filters on `kept`, not class), Stage 6 would DBSCAN it against a fallback epsilon and emit a nonsense L-shape box, Stage 8 would pass it through as `no_prior_for_class`, and `export_cvat_3d.py:203` (`label_id[row['class_name']]`, run at `fatal` severity) would abort the chain at publish time after every GPU stage had run. `eval_3d.py` would silently count it as false positives.
- *Not through the shared phrase taxonomy.* Every consumer of `Taxonomy.phrases` treats a phrase as an instance class with a 3D box footprint: `priors.py` emits a dims/eps_bev block per phrase, Stage 8 looks up a dimension prior, Stage 9 applies a 2× prior spatial gate, and `release_category_map.yaml` (18 classes, all things) refuses any unmapped phrase.
- *Not through `AnnotationRecord`.* It requires `instance_token`, strictly-positive `size_wlh_m`, a unit quaternion. `GroupAnnotation` (I-4G, polygon_bev_m + height_range_m) exists but is declared SHAPE ONLY with no producer and no consumer — the nearest existing hook, and it would need a producer.
- *Not through Stage 9's `drivable_term`.* Hard-locked to `"off"`; `GateConfig.validate()` refuses any other value, citing "§11 decision 7, locked at Phase 10 … requires a real map lookup plus no_production_counterpart marking — not a flag". Stage 9 is also not wired into `run_stages.sh` at all (verified: `ALL_STEPS=(0 1 3 4 5 6 7 8 eval viz cvat cvat3d)`, no `9` in the arg case) and has never run on this substrate.

**Where a road stage can go — freely.** Nothing in the repo enumerates `work_root`; every stage names its upstreams by explicit `--<name>-dir`. `boxes_dir()` only inspects stage7/stage6; `select_stage3_dir_for_4()` only inspects stage3*. A new sibling tree `<work_root>/stageNr_road` is **structurally invisible** to the existing chain — it cannot be picked up by accident. That makes a side-branch genuinely free.

**The measurement vacuum.** `sample_annotation.json`, `category.json`, `instance.json`, `attribute.json`, `visibility.json` are all 3 bytes (`[]`). No `maps/`, no lidarseg, no panoptic. `detect2d_metrics.json` reports 45,936 predictions against 0 GT and 0.0 precision everywhere; `detect3d_metrics.json` reports 23,627 boxes against 0 eligible GT; both scripts `return 0` and the run summary marks them `ok`. `paint_metrics.py` — the documented "single strongest correctness signal" — crashed on this run and wrote nothing. There is **no measurement of Dhaka output quality of any kind today**, and a road stage inherits exactly that.

### Tensions

- 2D-only versus lifted-to-3D. A 2D mask is cheap, reviewable in CVAT, and lands entirely inside existing machinery. Lifting it is NOT cheap: there is no unproject/ray-plane-intersection helper anywhere in the repo, §1.3 forbids reimplementing the projection chain, and the only plane available to intersect against is the one that sits 1.9 m above the road. The honest lift is Stage-5-style point painting of existing cloud points, which gives a SPARSE 3D road (limited to what the LiDAR returned and what survived ground removal) rather than a continuous surface. If a continuous BEV drivable surface is the product, approach C is the native answer and A/B are the wrong shape. Nobody has stated which is wanted.
- Extending the instance taxonomy versus a separate stuff class space. Every scout independently concluded 'do not touch the shared phrase taxonomy' — priors.py would emit a dims/eps_bev block for a road phrase, Stage 8 would look up a dimension prior, Stage 9 would apply a 2x-BEV spatial gate, release_category_map would refuse the export, and the CVAT exporter's sorted(set(phrases)) numbering would renumber existing category ids (measured: 'a road surface' renumbers 4, 'a drivable area' renumbers 8). But a separate class space means the road never appears in the same CVAT project as the objects, never reaches the release format, and needs its own everything. The counter-argument is real: if the deliverable is one coherent annotated dataset, two disjoint class spaces is a product decision disguised as an engineering one. The arm-B superset-sibling precedent shows how to do it if the answer is 'one space' — append only, never reorder.
- Side-branch versus inline integration. The side branch is genuinely free: verified that nothing enumerates work_root, boxes_dir() only inspects stage7/6, select_stage3_dir_for_4() only inspects stage3*. But 'free' also means 'disconnected' — the road output would reach no downstream consumer, no metric, no release artifact, and no QA gate. Inline integration is not merely harder, it is actively dangerous: a road region injected as a Stage 4 candidate is lifted by Stage 5, DBSCAN'd by Stage 6 against a fallback epsilon, tracked, inflated, gated, silently counted as false positives by eval_3d, and finally KeyErrors export_cvat_3d.py:203 at `fatal` severity after every GPU stage has run. The genuinely hard call is not which to pick — it is accepting that the side branch's disconnection is permanent unless someone reopens a locked decision.
- Whether quality can be measured at all here. It cannot, today, and the design must say so on the artifact rather than let a reader infer accuracy. There is no drivable-area GT, no lidarseg, no map expansion, no human labels, no held-out split (the probe reports the priors/tuning/run partition unsatisfiable with chunk_0000 unassigned), and the only human-corrected data anywhere is 420 decisions on three nuScenes-mini scenes. Worse, the existing metrics establish a BAD precedent: eval_2d and eval_3d return 0 and print 0.0% precision against a zero-size GT denominator, and run_stages.sh records them as `ok`. A road metric must distinguish 'measured zero' from 'nothing to measure against' — refuse, or emit gt_available:false, never 0.0. compare_runs.py's rule ('a cell missing a metric prints --, never 0.0') is the precedent to follow.
- Fixing Stage 1's ground fit versus building around it. Fixing it is the right engineering answer and would repair three downstream defects at once. But changing ground_band membership changes every downstream point count, num_lidar_pts, cluster and box in the run — invalidating every marker and fingerprint. Scout 1 asks directly whether the candidate-band/frame mismatch is a known accepted defect or an undiscovered one; no DECISIONS.md entry and no conformance.yaml row covers it (conformance flags the related height-cap-on-raw-z issue but not this). If it is undiscovered, fixing Stage 1 may be cheaper than building road geometry around a known-wrong plane — and that call is above a new stage's pay grade.
- SAM 3's off-label use versus a specialist model's licence encumbrance. SAM 3 is free, cached, commercially licensed, and has the best published number — but its own scope statement is about noun phrases denoting objects, and its road behaviour on Dhaka asphalt is inferred. Mask2Former-Mapillary has exactly the right vocabulary and is measured on driving benchmarks — but is CC BY-NC-SA and unmeasured on this hardware. There is no option that is both proven-on-task and licence-clean. Note the evidence AGAINST class-agnostic SAM is strong and specific (0.13-0.17 mIoU on road scenes, explicit over-segmentation of road under asphalt variation) — so the cached mobile_sam.pt is not a fallback, and SAM 3's concept prompt is doing all the work.
- Modal versus amodal road. Dhaka frames are heavily occluded by rickshaws and CNGs. A visible-road (modal) mask is a free-space product; an inferred-road-behind-vehicles (amodal) mask is a surface-extent product. These are different annotation products with different downstream contracts, and nobody has said which. It also determines whether export_cvat_coco's RETR_EXTERNAL hole-filling is a bug or a feature — it currently turns modal into pseudo-amodal by accident.
- Polygon versus RLE for CVAT. Polygons reuse mask_to_polygons verbatim (zero new code) but RETR_EXTERNAL fills holes, approxPolyDP(eps=1.0) plus a 20px floor discards thin slivers, a road boundary is far more vertex-hungry than an object outline, and a road polygon is the hardest shape to correct by vertex-dragging — which is the point of publishing it. COCO RLE arrives as a native editable CVAT MASK shape and COCO is the only import format here that also preserves per-annotation attributes — but needs a new encoder and adds weight to an already-102 MB single JSON. Two unverified sub-questions block this: whether CVAT's COCO importer accepts uncompressed vs pycocotools-compressed RLE counts, and whether iscrowd:1 is required. Neither scout could reach the live server or datumaro to confirm.
- Front-only versus all 8 cameras. 'The road AHEAD' reads as CAM_FRONT, which is 2,302 images and ~27 minutes; all 8 is 18,416 images and ~3.6 hours. This is the single biggest cost lever and it must be an explicit recorded config value, not an implicit loop. But note the rig's camera_priority list carries only 6 of 8 channels (CAM_LEFT/CAM_RIGHT absent, sorting to sentinel 99), and render_annotations.py's contact-sheet grid is hardcoded to the same 6 — so an all-8 road product is invisible in the view a reviewer actually scans.

## 9. Adversarial critique

> Note: this pass reviewed the synthesis above and **falsified several of its
> claims**. Where the two disagree, the critique cites measurements; prefer it,

### Gaps

- **The substrate is ONE continuous 230-second drive at 10 Hz — 1143.6 m of path, median 17.9 km/h, i.e. ~0.50 m of ego motion between consecutive keyframes (handover §4, verified). The synthesis treats 18,416 images as 18,416 independent segmentation problems and never mentions temporal redundancy, ego-motion, or propagation once.**
  - *why_it_matters*: Consecutive road masks are near-duplicates. Segmenting all 2,302 keyframes per camera independently buys almost nothing over segmenting every 10th and propagating, and it is the difference between a ~3.6 h stage and a ~20 min one. Worse, per-frame independent segmentation has no temporal-consistency guarantee at all, and road is the most temporally stable surface in the scene — so per-frame flicker becomes the dominant visible defect for a reviewer scrolling an 18,416-frame single CVAT job. The machinery already exists and is already paid for: Stage 3b runs SAM 3.1 video propagation for 6,131 s, and Stage 4's manifest records 'video propagation supported but not exercised' as a capability gap.
  - *how_to_close_it*: Make temporal stride and propagation an explicit, recorded config axis of the road stage, not an afterthought: segment every Nth keyframe with a text prompt and propagate through the tracker path, or segment every frame and add a temporal-consistency metric (IoU between consecutive road masks) as one of the very few quality numbers obtainable without ground truth. Measure both on a 200-keyframe slice before committing. Also record that ego z-drift of 15–20 m over the route (handover §4) forbids any scene-level accumulated road map.
- **Occlusion by dense traffic — the thing this dataset exists to capture — is not addressed, and the two mechanisms the synthesis leans on both fail for a road region. Verified: lift.py:64-65 states outright 'No occlusion reasoning. A mask is a 2D region; the far wall behind a car projects into the car's mask and is claimed by it.' Stage 6's mitigation is the 'keep largest cluster' reprojection-ghost filter (cluster.py:17-22), which has no meaning for a non-compact surface. And I measured that Stage 4's kept object masks cover only 1.1%–4.1% of each frame (per-camera means over all 2,302 keyframes) — so 'subtract vehicle pixels from the road mask' removes essentially nothing.**
  - *why_it_matters*: Point-painting a road mask through the existing chain is the WORST case for no-occlusion painting, not a benign one. Every 3D point along the ray through a road pixel — the road 30 m away, the underside of a bus, a wall beyond the road edge — lands inside the mask, and there is no largest-cluster fallback because the road is not a cluster. The synthesis's 'the honest lift is Stage-5-style point painting' is therefore not honest as written. Separately, the 1–4% object coverage is itself a finding: a detector that sees 2.5 objects per image in Dhaka traffic is missing most of the occluders, so any 'road minus vehicles' construction inherits the pipeline's own worst-known weakness ('Recall, not precision, is the weak point', handover §8.5).
  - *how_to_close_it*: State a road-specific occlusion policy explicitly: either (a) modal only, with per-point depth-ordering against the fitted ground surface as the ray-cull (a point is road only if its range matches the ground-plane intersection range for its pixel within a tolerance), or (b) declare the product amodal and stop at 2D. Do not inherit Stage 5's contest silently. And quantify the occluder-recall problem before promising any 'road minus objects' output.
- **Camera calibration quality is a first-order blocker for a road stage specifically, and appears only as a passing con under approach C. Verified in handover §8: six of eight camera pitches are ASSUMED (the mean of the two ZED-referenced cameras); CAM_FRONT_LEFT/RIGHT and CAM_BACK_LEFT/RIGHT have FOV-derived nominal intrinsics with no distortion model and principal point assumed at image centre (K = [[906.9,0,640],[0,906.9,360]]); three independent methods to recover the remaining pitches all failed on this data; and 'Run a target-based lidar-camera calibration before measuring anything from those six.'**
  - *why_it_matters*: A road surface is viewed at grazing incidence, so pitch error maps to range error with enormous gain — the exact opposite of a compact object at a known depth. The project already measured this: a 5.88° pitch error produced ~92 px of vertical error at fy≈953, and correcting the ZED extrinsic moved ground agreement from −0.89/−1.29 m to +0.01/+0.04 m. An uncalibrated pitch of even 1° puts the far edge of a 30 m road region metres wrong. So an 8-camera road product is a 2-camera product with six decorative channels, and any 3D road geometry derived from the six is unfalsifiable.
  - *how_to_close_it*: Scope the road stage to CAM_FRONT and CAM_BACK (the two ZED-referenced, distortion-modelled cameras) unless a target-based calibration is done first, and say so in the manifest as a capability gap. If all 8 are wanted, make the target-based calibration an explicit prerequisite step, not an assumption.
- **SAM 3's 1008×1008 square resize is not a design choice — it collides with a STANDING project tripwire. Verified: docs/DECISIONS.md:273 C13 (Status: STANDING) lists 'Square resizes forbidden'; docs/conformance.yaml:528 claims 'No square resize of non-square imagery anywhere; letterbox or resize-shortest-side only'. Verified in the cached checkpoint's processor_config.json: size {height: 1008, width: 1008}, default_to_square: true, mask_size {288, 288}.**
  - *why_it_matters*: The synthesis lists this as 'the design must choose letterbox vs. accept-distortion and record it', which reads as a free choice; it is a documented prohibition with a conformance row asserting compliance. Adopting approach A as written either violates C13 or falsifies conformance row 1.5-r3. It also matters more here than for objects: a road region is a wide shallow band, and 288×288 mask resolution over the ~200-px-tall road band means the road boundary is being decided at roughly 2.5 px of native vertical resolution.
  - *how_to_close_it*: Determine how Stage 4's existing transformers SAM 3 path is reconciled with C13 today (it appears to rely on post_process_masks(..., original_sizes) mapping back to 1280×720 rather than avoiding the square resize). Either (a) get C13 amended with that reading recorded, or (b) override the processor to letterbox. Either way this is a decision entry, not a config line. Separately: the synthesis states 'road occupies a wide shallow band where vertical squeeze matters most' — 1280×720→1008×1008 stretches vertically 1.40× and compresses horizontally 0.79×; the direction is inverted.
- **The ZED stereo passes — the best-characterised ground signal on this substrate — are treated only as contamination. Verified in handover §4/§5: ZED_FRONT/ZED_BACK are dense depth passes co-located with CAM_FRONT/CAM_BACK, ≤20 m, pitch-corrected, and their correction was VALIDATED specifically against lidar ground (agreement −0.89/−1.29 m → +0.01/+0.04 m). They contribute a median 81,483 of 101,535 points per keyframe. The scout's own measurement of the sampled keyframe's road band is 43,138 points, of which 4,511 are lidar and 38,627 are stereo — 89.5% stereo.**
  - *why_it_matters*: The synthesis says 'Silently fitting the fused cloud makes the road plane a stereo-calibration artifact within 20 m' and approach C therefore proposes fitting on the LiDAR-only accumulated cloud — which discards ~90% of the road-band evidence and is exactly backwards relative to the only ground-agreement measurement anyone has taken on this rig. ZED points also come with a native pixel correspondence in a co-located, distortion-modelled camera, sidestepping the six-assumed-pitch problem entirely.
  - *how_to_close_it*: Split the road-band point counts by ring tag over a proper sample (not one keyframe), and compare a plane fit on lidar-only vs stereo-only vs fused against each other. The ZED-vs-lidar ground agreement number already on record is the calibration evidence that decides this; use it rather than an a-priori 'stereo error grows as range squared' argument at ranges where the measurement says agreement is 4 cm.
- **Compute budget is misstated as 'a non-issue' against the wrong baseline. I read the actual run log: Stage 3b 6,131 s, Stage 4 3,562 s, Stage 5 151 s, Stage 6 8,260 s, Stage 7 7,909 s, Stage 8 5 s = 26,018 s ≈ 7.2 h for the chain. ### Missing Approach

Yes — a ZED-stereo-first road surface, using the two co-located depth passes as the primary signal instead of treating stereo as contamination. All three proposed approaches either ignore ZED (A, B) or explicitly exclude it (C fits on the LiDAR-only accumulated cloud). But ZED_FRONT/ZED_BACK are dense near-field depth passes (~81k of 101k points per keyframe, ≤20 m — exactly the band the road occupies), they are co-located with CAM_FRONT and CAM_BACK, and — decisively — their extrinsic correction was validated SPECIFICALLY against lidar ground agreement, moving from −0.89/−1.29 m to +0.01/+0.04 m (handover §4). That is the only ground-referenced calibration measurement anyone has on this rig, and it makes ZED the best-characterised ground signal available, not the worst. Three properties nobody exploited: (1) a ZED point has a native pixel correspondence in its co-located camera, so a road surface built from ZED and painted into CAM_FRONT/CAM_BACK never touches the six assumed-pitch, nominal-intrinsic cameras and never re-runs the 4-hop projection chain; (2) at ~40k points per camera in the 0–20 m band it is ~9× denser than lidar in the road band on the one keyframe measured (38,627 stereo vs 4,511 lidar), so a surface fit is far better conditioned than the 8-sector RANSAC that is currently failing 76.4% of the time; (3) it is separable for free — provenance already rides in the ring column (10/11), no schema change, no re-read of the dataroot. The natural shape is: fit a road/ground surface per keyframe from ring-10/11 points inside the co-located camera's frustum, use it as the depth-ordering falsifier for a SAM 3 road mask in that same camera (a road pixel's mask claim is accepted only if a stereo point at that pixel lies on the fitted surface — which is also the occlusion policy gap above), and publish the agreement statistic between the two independent estimates as the stage's headline quality number. That last part is what makes it materially different rather than a variant: it is the only proposal that yields a real, falsifiable quality metric on a substrate with zero ground truth, because it cross-checks two independent sensing modalities against each other rather than reporting self-consistency. It also directly follows the handover's own instruction to compare against ZED before trusting anything geometric on this rig. Its honest limits: ≤20 m range and front/back only, so it is a near-field, two-camera product — but on this rig that is what the calibration actually supports.
